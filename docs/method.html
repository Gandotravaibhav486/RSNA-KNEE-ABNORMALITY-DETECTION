Method note · RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection

Running experiments when you only have 58 labels

How this pipeline is built, measured, and judged — and why the measuring instrument turned out to matter more than the model.

The constraint everything follows from

The competition ships 4,407 knee MRI studies with twelve findings each. 58 of them carry real radiologist annotations. The other 4,349 carry a free-text report and nothing else — and reports exist for 100% of train and 0% of test, so text can only ever manufacture targets, never feed the model.

Two consequences shape every decision below. Almost everyone is training against labels they invented, so label quality is a ceiling no encoder can pass. And 58 studies is a very short ruler: on it, the two-sigma bar for calling a change real is 0.1110 AUC — wider than most changes worth making.

Three instruments, and what each is for

We never report one number. Every change is read against whichever instrument can actually resolve it, and the gold set is reserved as a confirmation surface rather than a search surface.

Pairing is worth roughly four times the sample size and costs nothing — it only requires keeping every run's predictions on disk.

The screening metric earned its place by prediction, not by being tighter. For the geometry change, gold said +0.0154, screening said +0.0082, and the leaderboard delivered +0.005. Gold overstated by 3×; screening landed within 0.003. It is not merely narrower — it appears less biased.

What the screening metric is not. It measures agreement with model-generated labels whose pooled false-positive rate we measured at 0.66. Its absolute value is never quoted as a score, and it is blind to any improvement on findings the reports never mention.

How an experiment is created

This is a genuine sequence — each step gates the next, and the expensive step sits deliberately late.

  1. IntakeA proposal must name its mechanism and its kill criterion before anything is built. Competition discussions and top public notebooks are read first; several of our experiments were killed or reframed by someone else's published measurement.
  2. Plan, if it costs GPUHypothesis, expected delta, GPU hours, and what result would end it — written down and approved before the first epoch. Anything answerable by arithmetic is answered by arithmetic instead.
  3. Build as a notebook variantGenerated from the current baseline by explicit patch, so the only difference from the control is the thing under test. Versioned on two axes: p1/p2 keys the tensor cache, t1/t2 keys the training stream.
  4. Static lintpyflakes across the whole notebook before it is allowed near a GPU. Local runs skip every image-dependent branch, so undefined names hide there until they cost an hour.
  5. Preflight, in codeThe run raises before epoch one if the weights or the cache did not mount. The kill criterion lives in the notebook, not in a document — the document version cost 1.26 GPU hours of training from random initialisation.
  6. Run, isolatedOne Kaggle kernel per experiment, two GPU sessions concurrently, CPU work pushed to CPU kernels where no quota applies.
  7. Score paired, offlineEvery run saves its predictions. The comparison against the control is a paired bootstrap computed locally at zero cost — which is how a result that gold called unresolved became a confirmed +1.
  8. Verdict and ledgerOne row, including the rejected and the absorbed, so no idea is proposed twice.

Verdicts

+1 clears 2σ on the instrument that can see it — pursue, and design follow-ups on the same mechanism. −1 is inside the noise or worse — stop, and record why. null is no measurable effect; a correctness fix is adopted anyway, but no gain is claimed from it. A run that broke a precondition is invalid and counts as evidence for nothing.

The baseline itself only moves on a separate gate: a 20% closure of the error gap, or a genuinely new approach family, which gets its own lineage rather than replacing the old one.

Where the model still lags its own labels

The remaining shortfall is concentrated, not diffuse. Against the label ceiling on the same 58 studies, three targets carry 0.74 of the total 1.01 gap:

Ligaments and menisci are small, focal, and visible in a handful of slices in one plane; effusion and osteoarthritis are diffuse and visible almost anywhere in the volume. We sample three windows per slot — thin coverage exactly where a meniscal tear lives. That is a testable geometry hypothesis, not a shrug.

Failure modes, all paid for once

Every failure in this programme so far has died after the expensive part, never before it, and none of them raised an error. They produced plausible numbers from wrong inputs.

  • Silent fallback to random initialisation. Attachments did not mount; training ran an hour and logged a believable score. Now a preflight assertion.
  • A missing import after six folds of training. Saved predictions meant the analysis was redone locally for free. Now a pre-push lint.
  • Stale artifacts. The Kaggle client skips files that already exist locally, so a re-fetch served a previous version's predictions — producing an estimate twelve times too large, caught only because it was implausible.
  • Silent no-op edits. String replacements against anchors that had already changed dropped ten of twenty-two rows from the ledger. Edits now assert that the anchor existed.
The through-line. Cheap guards at the front — a lint, an assertion, a saved prediction file — are worth more than careful reading at the back. Every one of these was invisible to an execution-based check and obvious to a static one.
