## Body — tightened (≈340 words, was ≈620)

Hi all,

I'm Vaibhav Gandotra, 2020 batch, Pilani — MSc Economics + BE EEE. I'm a Quant Analyst at UBS now.

I went through the same placement process you're about to. What caught me out wasn't the technical prep. It was that I'd never practised **speaking** under that kind of pressure.

So I've been building **mockintervew.com** — an AI that runs a real back-and-forth mock interview. It asks a question, listens to your answer, then asks a follow-up based on what you actually said. Not a question list. It reacts.

**Why it might be worth 15 minutes.**

Everyone grinds DSA, core subjects, formulas. Almost nobody practises saying any of it out loud to someone who's judging them. Then you sit in the actual interview and find out that knowing the answer and delivering it are different skills.

That's what this scores. Correctness is 25% of the grade. The other 75% — clarity, delivery, posture, presence — is how you come across, and you get written notes on each: where you rambled, where you hedged, where you trailed off.

**The ask:** one full interview, about 15 minutes. Pick the role you're actually targeting, and **finish it** — especially if it's going badly. A session abandoned halfway tells me almost nothing; a finished bad one tells me everything.

→ **mockintervew.com**

**Before you start, four honest things:**

- **It's early.** You'll hit rough edges. I'm asking campus rather than launching publicly precisely because I want failures reported, not silently abandoned.
- **It uses your camera and mic.** Two of the five scores come from video. Nothing is visible to anyone but you — I can see that a session happened and where it stopped, nothing more.
- **Trust the verbal scores over the visual ones.** Posture and Attire are partly tracking how much you talk rather than how you present. That's wrong and I'm fixing it. Read them as rough.
- **No role listed?** Paste the job description instead — that path generates questions from the posting. Quant roles have no question bank yet, so use the JD option there.

Use whatever device you'd normally use. Don't switch to a laptop on my account — if it breaks on your phone, that's exactly what I need to find out.

**One request.** Because I'm an alum there's a decent chance you'll be polite about this. Please don't be. If it's confusing, slow, or the questions are irrelevant, say so plainly. That's far more useful than encouragement, and you won't offend me.

And if it's a bad week, ignore this. No hard feelings.

Separately, and regardless of whether you try the tool: if you have questions about quant roles, the UBS process, or placements generally, reach out. Happy to help.

Best,
Vaibhav Gandotra
2020 batch, Pilani

## What changed and why

- **Cut ~45%.** Most of it read on a phone; the original ran long enough that the ask sat below three scrolls. The CTA now appears at roughly the halfway point.
- **One CTA, stated once, as a link on its own line.** The original mentioned the URL twice with no visual break.
- **Caveats turned into a scannable list.** They are the most trust-building part of the email — but as prose they read as hedging. As a bulleted "four honest things" block they read as candour.
- **Kept verbatim:** the "finish it even if it's going badly" ask, the "please don't be polite" close, and the "if it's a bad week, ignore this" out. Those are the email's real assets.
- **Removed:** "The follow-up questions are the part I'd most like tested" — it duplicated the intro paragraph's point.
